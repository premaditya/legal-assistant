Question 1: What are the essential elements of a valid contract under the Indian Contract Act, 1872?

Answer:
Based on the provided context, under **Section 10** of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, all agreements are contracts if they meet the following essential elements:

1. **Free Consent:** The agreement must be made by the free consent of the parties (Section 10, p. 13). Consent is defined when two or more persons agree upon the same thing in the same sense (Section 13). Consent is considered "free" when it is not caused by coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, or mistake (Section 14).
2. **Competency of the Parties:** The parties must be competent to contract (Section 10, p. 13). Under Section 11, a person is competent to contract if:
   - They have attained the age of majority according to the law to which they are subject;
   - They are of sound mind; and
   - They are not disqualified from contracting by any law to which they are subject (Section 11, p. 13).
3. **Lawful Consideration:** The agreement must be supported by a lawful consideration (Section 10, p. 13).
4. **Lawful Object:** The agreement must be made for a lawful object (Section 10, p. 13).
5. **Not Expressly Declared Void:** The agreement must not be expressly declared to be void under the Act (Section 10, p. 13).

**Formalities/Exceptions:** 
Section 10 also states that this provision does not affect any existing law requiring an agreement to be made in writing, in the presence of witnesses, or laws concerning the registration of documents (Section 10, p. 13).

Question 7: What is the difference between fraud and misrepresentation under the Indian Contract Act?

Answer:
Based on the provided context, the differences between fraud and misrepresentation under the Indian Contract Act include:

### 1. Intent to Deceive and Belief in the Truth
* **Misrepresentation:** Occurs without an intention to deceive. It includes:
  * Making a statement of that which is not true, though the person making it believes it to be true.
  * Any breach of duty committed *without an intent to deceive* that gains an advantage by misleading another.
  * Causing, *however innocently*, a party to an agreement to make a mistake as to the substance of the subject of the agreement.
* **Fraud:** Involves an intent to deceive or induce another party to enter into a contract (as seen in Section 19, Illustration (a)). It includes:
  * Suggesting as a fact that which is not true by someone who *does not believe it to be true*.
  * Active concealment of a fact by someone with knowledge or belief of it.
  * A promise made without any intention of performing it.
  * Any other act fitted to deceive, or acts/omissions declared fraudulent by law.
  * Silence where there is a duty to speak, or where silence is equivalent to speech (Explanation to fraud definition; Section 19 Exception).

### 2. Means of Discovering the Truth (Ordinary Diligence)
* Under the **Exception to Section 19**, if consent was caused by **misrepresentation** (or by silence that is fraudulent within the meaning of Section 17), the contract is **not voidable** if the party whose consent was caused had the means of discovering the truth with ordinary diligence. 
* By contrast, this ordinary diligence exception does not apply to other active forms of fraud.

### **Definition of Cheating**
Under **Section 318(1)**, a person is said to cheat if they, by deceiving any person:
1. **Fraudulently or dishonestly induce** the deceived person:
   - To deliver any property to any person, or
   - To consent that any person shall retain any property; **OR**
2. **Intentionally induce** the deceived person to do or omit to do anything which they would not otherwise do or omit, where that act or omission causes or is likely to cause damage or harm to that person in body, mind, reputation, or property.

* **Explanation:** A dishonest concealment of facts constitutes deception under this section (Section 318(1)).

---

### **Punishment for Cheating**
The context outlines three levels of punishment depending on the nature of the offence:

2. **Cheating by a Person Bound to Protect Interests:**
   Whoever cheats knowing that their act is likely to cause wrongful loss to a person whose interest they were legally or contractually bound to protect shall be punished with imprisonment of either description for a term that may extend to **five years**, or with fine, or with both (Section 318(3)).

3. **Cheating and Dishonestly Inducing Delivery of Property / Valuable Security:**
   Whoever cheats and thereby dishonestly induces the deceived person:
   - To deliver any property to any person, or
